02 — Access Control

Granular, auditable access.
No user sees more than they should.

Flourish uses a tuple-based Role + Scope permission model — more precise than traditional RBAC. Every user accumulates permission pairs that define exactly what they can do and where.

👁
View
Read-only access to content, claims, and references within the user's scope.
✏️
Manage
Create, edit, and delete within scope. Includes uploading references and drafting content.
✅
Approve
Authorise state transitions — e.g. move a claim from In Review to Approved. Legal teams only.
Example: A Reckitt regional approver for Germany has exactly two permission tuples:
(Role: Viewer) + (Scope: Region.EMEA, Product.All) (Role: Approver) + (Scope: Region.Germany, Product.All)
This user can view content across EMEA — but can only approve content scoped to Germany. Pet Nutrition and Royal Canin data are separate scopes; neither leaks to the other.
What this means for Reckitt
  • Pet Nutrition data is inaccessible to Royal Canin users — by architecture, not policy
  • Market-level approvers cannot approve content outside their market
  • Every access action is logged and attributable
  • User group changes take effect immediately
Scope dimensions
  • Region — Global → EMEA → UK, Germany, etc.
  • Product — Brand → Product line → SKU
  • Sector — Pet Nutrition, Healthcare, etc.
  • Pillar — Sustainability, Science, Heritage

04 — AI Governance

Your data is yours.
It never trains a model.

The question every enterprise IT team asks — answered plainly.

The core commitment
Client Vault data — brand standards, claim libraries, product specifications, approved content — is stored in Flourish's own infrastructure. It is never ingested into, stored by, or used to train any foundation AI model. Flourish operates under API agreements with Anthropic and other AI providers that explicitly prohibit use of customer data for model training.
Which AI models does Flourish use?
Anthropic Claude — primary reasoning, content generation, and compliance analysis
OpenRouter — model routing layer; enables best-model-for-task selection and provider redundancy
What is sent to AI models?
✓ The content being reviewed or generated
✓ Relevant claims from the client Vault (task-scoped)
✓ Applicable regulatory rules for the task
✗ PII — not included by design
✗ Full Vault — only task-relevant context is passed
✗ Unpublished proprietary data beyond task scope
📋
Audit trail on every AI action. Every AI-assisted operation in Flourish produces a permanent record: what was generated, which claims it referenced, which regulations were applied, who reviewed it, and when. If a claim is ever challenged, the full chain of evidence is already written — traceable to a primary source, defensible in court.
Data sovereignty — the Vault
The client Vault belongs to the client. On contract end, Vault data is fully exportable. Flourish does not retain it beyond the contractual period. It never contributes to Flourish's own models or any third-party foundation model.
Sub-processors
Full sub-processor list available on request. Key sub-processors: Google Cloud Platform (infrastructure), Bubble (application), Pinecone (vector search), Anthropic (AI), Cloudflare (network security). All operate under formal data processing agreements.

What we found

The Dutch was excellent everywhere. The risk was invisible to almost everyone.

The US email spoke to readers "considering GLP-1s." Perfectly legal in America. Every model translated it faithfully into Dutch — and in doing so, walked straight into European law.

The source line · US English
"Whether you're managing diabetes, considering GLP-1s, or navigating menopause, our plans can help you get healthier."
Leading frontier model · faithful translation
"Of je nu diabetes onder controle houdt, GLP-1's overweegt of door de overgang gaat, onze plannen helpen je gezonder te worden."
Cannot be sent in the Netherlands
Flourish · governed output
Flags the line as high-risk, then rewrites it compliantly: "Of je nu werkt aan je gezondheid, je energie of een routine die je écht volhoudt — onze plannen helpen je gezonder te worden."
Cleared to ship

GLP-1 medicines are prescription-only. Under EU Directive 2001/83/EC (Article 88), advertising prescription medicines to the public is prohibited across the EU — fully harmonised, no exceptions. Direct-to-consumer prescription-drug advertising is legal in essentially just two countries on earth: the US and New Zealand. The source line was lawful only because it was written for one of them. Translate it word-for-word into Dutch and you have an unlawful advertisement — fluent, on-brand, and un-sendable.

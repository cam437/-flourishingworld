Comparing the models

Every AI can write Dutch. Only one knew what was in line with Dutch audience insights and legal to send.

We gave the CRM copy to ChatGPT and Claude and asked the same questions we asked Flourish: “Can you translate and localise this copy?” and “Could you say this in the Netherlands?”

Both models produced fluent, natural Dutch. Neither of them caught the legal problems.

The US email spoke to readers “considering GLP-1s,” which is perfectly legal in America. Every model translated it faithfully into Dutch, and broke European advertising law in the process.

The source line · US English
“Whether you’re managing diabetes, considering GLP-1s, or navigating menopause, our plans can help you get healthier.”
Leading frontier model · faithful translation
“Of je nu diabetes onder controle houdt, GLP-1’s overweegt of door de overgang gaat, onze plannen helpen je gezonder te worden.”
Cannot be sent in the Netherlands
Flourish · governed output
Flags the line as high-risk, then rewrites it compliantly: “Of je nu werkt aan je gezondheid, je energie of een routine die je écht volhoudt — onze plannen helpen je gezonder te worden.”
Defensible and ready to send

GLP-1 medicines are prescription-only. Under EU Directive 2001/83/EC (Article 88), advertising prescription medicines to the public is prohibited across all 27 EU member states. Direct-to-consumer prescription-drug advertising is legal in two countries: the US and New Zealand. The source line was lawful only because it was written for one of them. A word-for-word Dutch translation is an unlawful advertisement.

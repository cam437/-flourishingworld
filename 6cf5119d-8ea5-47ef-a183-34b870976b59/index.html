The reframe

We are not selling software. We are selling the function.

Internalise this and every conversation changes. The budget, the buyer, the benchmark — all of them shift.

The one paragraph to carry into every call

For every pound a business spends on software, roughly six pounds go on the services that wrap around it. Legal review. Agency compliance passes. Regulatory consultancy. Localisation QA. Recall remediation. The SaaS playbook captured the software pound. The AI playbook captures the services pounds, at software margins.

Flourish solves this with the Vault — a structured record of every approved claim, its supporting evidence, the approval that cleared it, and the regulatory context it applies to. Legal stops investigating from zero. Marketing stops waiting.

The discovery question

The single most valuable question you can ask.

Learn it. Use it early. Use it on every call.

Ask this

"Across your organisation, what do you currently spend on marketing legal review, agency compliance passes, regulatory consultancy, and content remediation in a typical year?"

Nobody has the number to hand. That's the point. The question forces the buyer to do the mental maths in front of you, and the maths always lands in seven figures for a serious brand. Once that number is in the room, the conversation about our commercial model gets much easier — because you're no longer anchored to a £50k SaaS line item. You're anchored to a £2m services line item that Flourish replaces.

If they resist a number, reframe: "Rough order of magnitude? More like £500k, £2m, or £10m?" Let them pick the bracket. The bracket is what you need.

For the CMO

"How much of your marketing budget currently goes to getting content through legal and compliance, rather than to the content itself? What percentage would you estimate?"

For the CLO / General Counsel

"How many hours a month does your team spend reviewing marketing claims? How much of that review is catching the same kinds of issues you've caught before?"

For the CIO / IT Security

"What is the cost, in risk and engineering time, of your marketing teams using public AI tools you cannot govern? What is your current exposure?"

For the CFO / Procurement

"Across your top five external legal and consultancy retainers, what share of that spend is marketing compliance? What line would it fall under if we consolidated it?"

Questions you'll be asked

Handle these with confidence.

The most common objections — and how to answer them cleanly.

Isn't this just a thin wrapper on GPT?
No. The Vault, the regulatory database, and the approval workflow exist independently of any LLM. The intelligence layer — 50 markets of jurisdictionally precise regulatory data, built over three years — is proprietary. The LLM is a surface, not the product. Flourish without GPT still works. GPT without Flourish produces nothing defensible.
Why not just build it internally?
Two major global FMCG brands tried it — both found it expensive to build and incomplete within a year as regulations shifted. The regulatory intelligence layer alone took Flourish three years to build and requires continuous maintenance. An internal build starts behind and falls further behind every quarter. Flourish is already built, already updated, and already proven.
Is our data safe? Does it train your models?
Client Vault data is sovereign. It never trains any foundation model — not Flourish's, not anyone else's. Full export on exit. The Vault belongs to the client. Data residency options available — route specifics to the team.
We already use GPT / Copilot. Why Flourish?
GPT has no memory of your brand's previous approvals. Every output is generated fresh with no audit trail. If a regulator asks why a claim was made, GPT can't answer. Flourish output is defensible because every output traces back to an approved claim in the Vault. Flourish doesn't replace GPT — it governs it.
How quickly do we see value?
Weeks, not months. Onboarding is service-led — Flourish populates the Vault from your existing materials (previous campaigns, existing approvals, brand guidelines). You don't start from zero. You start from everything you've already built.
What does implementation look like?
Browser-based. No enterprise IT project required. Flourish integrates with existing workflows rather than replacing them. Start in days, not months. Microsoft Teams and Copilot integration available for organisations already in the M365 ecosystem.
How does this compare to Writer or Jasper?
Writer and Jasper are productivity tools for marketers — they help a marketer write faster. Flourish is the governance infrastructure that sits across marketing, legal, and compliance. Your marketers might well use a copilot to draft. Flourish is what makes sure what they draft is defensible, substantiated, approved, and auditable before it ever goes to market. Different layer. Different buyer. Different budget.
Is this per-seat or per-user?
Flourish is priced by governed decision, because that's the unit of value we produce. Seats don't reflect what we do. A single marketing decision — substantiated, approved, shipped — is worth far more than a login. It's also how you're already paying for this work externally, just to different vendors. We're replacing that spend, not adding to it. If commercial terms need to flex for procurement, they can. The positioning does not. Route commercials to Maddy or Andy.
How does this fit into our SaaS budget category?
It doesn't, and that's the point. If you categorise Flourish as marketing SaaS, you'll try to fund it out of a martech budget that's probably already stretched, and you'll benchmark us against tools that do a fraction of what we do. If you categorise Flourish correctly — as governance infrastructure replacing external services spend — the budget source is different, the business case is different, the approval path is different, and the value is orders of magnitude clearer. Most of our enterprise contracts sit in a consolidated governance or compliance infrastructure line, not in martech.
What happens when Microsoft or Salesforce builds this?
They build horizontal productivity. We build the governance vault for regulated marketing. The vault is the moat. Every day a brand uses Flourish, their vault gets more valuable, more accurate, more defensible. That compounding asset is not something a horizontal vendor can replicate — they don't have the per-brand structure or the regulatory depth. Veeva proved this in pharma at £2bn in annual revenue. The model works when the vault is the product.
How do we know the AI won't make mistakes on compliance?
Flourish is not generating compliance decisions in isolation. Every claim is grounded in a reference. Every reference has a substantiation link with a specific passage or data point. Every approval is a human action, recorded and audited. The AI proposes, flags, and accelerates. Humans decide and approve. The audit trail shows every step. That's structurally different from asking a general-purpose AI to tell you if a claim is compliant — which is what most organisations are worried about, rightly.
What if we're not ready for this yet?
The AI Act Article 50 deadline is August 2026. The Product Liability Directive lands in December 2026. Whatever capability you have in place by then is what you'll be audited against. "Not ready" is not a position that survives contact with a regulator. The question isn't whether you adopt this capability — it's whether you adopt it deliberately now, or reactively under pressure later.
What not to say

The lines that matter.

Flourish operates at the intersection of marketing and compliance. Words carry weight here.

⚠ Do not say these things

✗
Don't call it "AI for compliance." It's brand intelligence infrastructure. The framing matters — compliance suggests a review layer. Flourish is a system of record that makes compliance a byproduct of normal operation.
✗
Don't say it replaces GPT or Copilot. It governs them. Flourish is the layer that makes AI tools safe and defensible — not a competitor to the tools your prospects are already using.
✗
Don't make up pricing. Route all pricing conversations to Maddy or Andy. Pricing is contextual — enterprise deals, agency models, and mid-market challenger brands are all different conversations.
✗
Don't promise specific SLAs, uptime figures, or implementation timelines. Route to the team for anything specific. General framing (weeks, not months; hours, not days) is fine — specific numbers are not yours to give.
✗
Don't say Flourish is always right. Say it's always evidenced and auditable. The value isn't infallibility — it's that every decision is traceable and defensible. That's a much stronger claim.
✗
Don't sell features when you could sell outcomes. The feature-language column of the guide is not for use on calls. Every time you reach for a technical description of what the system does, reach for the thing the buyer stops instead.
